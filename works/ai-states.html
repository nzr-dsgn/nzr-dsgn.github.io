01 · LATENCY BANDS

The wait decides the pattern

A request that takes a quarter of a second needs no loader at all. One that takes ten seconds needs progress and a way out. The marker follows the latency slider above.

nonespinnerstep list+ exit 3.0 s
00.4 s2 s8 s12 s
UNDER 0.4 S

Show nothing

A spinner that appears and vanishes inside half a second reads as a glitch. The result just arrives.

0.4 TO 2 S

Spinner and a verb

One line, present tense: “Checking calendars”. The person learns what is happening without having to read a list.

2 TO 8 S

Step list

Each step has its own state, so a long wait is divided into short, visible progress. Cancel stays in reach.

OVER 8 S

Steps and an exit

Admit the delay, say they can leave, and show partial results as they arrive. Silence is the thing that feels slow.

Words for AI statuses

Status text is interface, not decoration. Each line below says what the system is doing to which thing, and what the person can do next.

Rule 1

Present tense while working

“Checking calendars.” The system is doing it now, so the verb says so.

Rule 2

Past tense when done

“Room B2 booked.” The result is a fact, and the sentence reports it as one.

Rule 3

Name the object and the next step

“Room B2” beats “it”. “Retry, or book without invites” beats “try again later”.

04 · MOTION

Four durations, three curves

Motion explains what changed. Press or hover a curve to run it. The ball underneath runs at real speed with the actual token; the dot on the curve runs five times slower so you can follow it. The 0.25× switch in the lab dock also slows these.

--d1 · 100 msPress feedback. Buttons, chips.
--d2 · 180 msHover and small changes. Step ticks.
--d3 · 280 msEnter. New blocks, progress width.
--d4 · 440 msLayout and commit. Toast, card morph.

With reduced motion on, durations drop to zero and every curve shows its end state. The lab still runs on time; only the movement is removed.

05 · NEW COMPONENTS

Four proposals for the system

Everything below is built from Brightyard tokens and sits beside the existing AI language. All four are live.

Step list

Agent progress for waits over two seconds. Each step carries its own state and a line of detail.

States: pending, active, done, failed, skipped

Intent chips

The parsed request, shown before work starts. Press a chip to fix a misread. Assumptions say so.

States: default, hover, editing, ambiguous, assumed

Undo countdown

A toast action with a ring that shows how long the undo lasts. When it ends, the toast leaves and the result stays.

States: counting, undone, expired

Latency-aware loader

One component, four faces. It picks the face from the expected wait, so product teams do not choose a spinner by habit.

States: none, inline, steps, steps with exit

06 · WHAT I WOULD TEST

Three hypotheses

None of these has been tested. They are the questions I would put to real people first.

H1 · The 0.4 s rule

Silence feels faster

I expect that showing no loader under 0.4 s makes quick answers feel faster and prompts no extra “did it work?” taps. Compare it with a spinner that always shows, measuring perceived speed and repeat taps.

H2 · Intent chips

Misreads get fixed early

I expect that people who see the parsed request as chips fix a wrong time or head count before the proposal appears, so fewer proposals need Change. Count edits per run and Change taps per proposal.

H3 · Undo ring

A visible clock helps, or pressures

I expect people to trust a ring more than a bare Undo link. It could also rush them into undoing just to stop the timer. Watch for Undo taps in the last two seconds.
