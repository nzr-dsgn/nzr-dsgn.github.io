    THE BRIEF

    Start with a voice note, end with a decision

    Product owners often brief by voice. I turn the message into goals, rules and questions before drawing anything.

    "For the tenant portal, the main thing is people waste time on small stuff: booking a room, adding a visitor, parking. I want an assistant they can just tell what they need. But I don't want it doing things on its own. People must always confirm. It should work on phones too. And facilities requests, like 'it's cold on the fourth floor'. Can you show me a couple of options for how the assistant shows up?"
    Voice note · 0:42 · transcript written by me as an example brief
    Goals

    Save time on small tasks

    Rooms, visitors, parking and facilities requests, each in a few taps, on web and on a phone.

    Rules I extracted

    The tenant always decides

    No action without a visible confirmation. Every change is previewed first and can be undone.

    Questions I would ask

    Before it goes further

    Who pays for a booking that costs money? What happens when facilities is offline? Which languages must it understand?

    THREE DIRECTIONS

    How should the assistant show up?

    I asked the agent for three layouts of the same dashboard and compared them by what the tenant is doing at that moment.

    A · Command bar and drawer · chosen

    One entry point, results stay visible

    +The dashboard stays on screen, so the tenant sees the booking land.
    +The same entry point works with a keyboard shortcut.
    −Less discoverable than a visible button, so the bar says what to ask.
    B · Floating chat bubble

    Familiar, but it invites open chat

    +Everyone knows the pattern.
    −Covers content on a phone and promises answers the assistant can't give.
    −Hides what is changing behind a chat window.
    C · Suggestions only

    Quiet and safe, but passive

    +No empty chat box to fill, nothing to misunderstand.
    −The tenant can't ask for what they need right now.
    +Worth keeping as a card next to A.

    Decision: A, plus C's suggestions as a dashboard card. The tenant can ask, and the product can also notice. Both paths end in the same confirmation.

    THE RULES

    Five rules the assistant follows

    These rules are part of the design system, so every future AI feature inherits them.

    Dashed means proposal, solid means done

    A violet dashed outline marks something the AI suggests. Once the tenant confirms, the card turns solid. The state is readable at a glance.

    ProposalBook Room B2
    DoneRoom B2 booked

    Show the evidence

    Every proposal says why. Three short reasons give the tenant something to agree or disagree with, instead of a verdict from nowhere.

    Fits 8 peopleSame buildingFree for the whole hour

    Based on your calendar only. No other tenant's data was used.

    Preview the change

    Before anything is booked or sent, the card lists what will change and what will not.

    When
    Today · 14:00–15:00
    Where
    Room B2 · floor 2
    Invites
    Not sent

    Undo stays within reach

    After confirming, an undo control stays visible with a countdown ring. No hidden menus, no support ticket.

    Room B2 booked. Undo for a few more seconds.

    Failure says what happened to the data

    The first thing an error tells the tenant is whether anything was changed. Then it offers a way forward.

    Not sentFacilities didn't respond

    Nothing was sent. Your request is still here.

    WHAT I WOULD TEST NEXT

    Hypotheses, not claims

    This concept has not been tested with tenants. This is how I would find out whether it works.

    H1 · SPEED

    Tenants confirm a booking faster with the assistant than with the booking form.

    Measure: time from intent to confirmed booking, on 3 tasks, with 5 tenants.
    H2 · TRUST

    Showing why a room was chosen raises acceptance without slowing the decision.

    Measure: proposal acceptance rate and time to decide, with and without evidence chips.
    H3 · SAFETY

    An 8-second undo window is long enough for tenants to catch a wrong proposal.

    Measure: undo rate, and wrong bookings found later through support requests.
