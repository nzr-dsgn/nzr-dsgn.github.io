04 · HOW I COMPARED THEM

Seven questions, three answers

My own judgement from clicking through the prototypes. Nothing here is measured.

QuestionA · QueueB · Book a slotC · Smart match
Taps to get a charger ✓ Good2 taps: join, then "I'm on my way". ! Risk3 taps and a grid to scan. ✓ Good1 tap to accept, when the match is right.
What if you're late ✓ Good10-minute hold, then it passes on. ! RiskThe slot sits empty until the grace time ends. ✓ GoodSame hold as A once you accept.
What if the system is wrong ✓ GoodOne simple rule. Easy to see why. ✓ GoodConflicts show up and offer an alternative. ! RiskA wrong guess costs trust. Needs a clear way out.
Fairness ✓ GoodFirst come, first served. ! RiskPlanners grab the best slots early. ! RiskRules are hidden. Hard to explain.
Who is in control ✓ GoodThe driver, with one rule. ✓ GoodThe driver, but must plan ahead. ! RiskThe assistant decides, the driver confirms.
New components needed ✓ Good2: queue position card, countdown ring. ! Risk2 and both are heavy: slot grid, conflict sheet. ✓ GoodReuses .by-ai, adds quick-reply chips.
Build effort ✓ GoodSmall. A queue service and push messages. ! RiskLarge. Calendar logic, conflicts, no-shows. ! RiskLarge. A prediction model, and A as the fallback.
My pick

A as the base. C only as an optional hint. B dropped.

  • The rule fits one sentence. "First in line gets the next free charger" is easy to trust and easy to explain to a facility manager.
  • C's value is a small part of C. The finish-time estimate helps in A's waiting state. The rest needs a model the garage does not have, and it names another tenant's car.
  • B asks people to plan. Drivers who are done charging are not planners. Late arrivals leave idle chargers, which is the problem we started with.

07 · WHAT I WOULD TEST

Three hypotheses, not results

Nobody has tested this yet. This is how I would find out.

Hypothesis 1

"3rd · about 25 min" is clear on its own.

Five tenants, think-aloud on prototype A. Ask each person what happens next. Count the ones who guess wrong.

Hypothesis 2

A 10-minute hold feels fair.

Role-play with five drivers at 5, 10 and 15 minutes, once as the holder and once as the person waiting. Compare how each felt.

Hypothesis 3

A neutral notice moves cars as fast as a streak nudge.

A two-week pilot in P2 with one message each. Measure minutes between 100% and unplugging. Watch for complaints.

08 · NEW COMPONENTS

What I added to the system

Built on by.css tokens. All of them are live below.

Voice note player

Plays a recorded message with a waveform and a synced transcript. States: idle, playing, paused, seeking, compact.

Countdown ring

Shows a hold that is running out. The ring, the number and a word all change, so colour is never the only signal. States: running, last minute, expired.

Queue position card

Answers "where am I and how long?" in one glance. States: waiting, next, your turn.

Charger slot tile

One of six in the strip. Fill height shows battery level. States: charging, done, free, yours, next to free.
