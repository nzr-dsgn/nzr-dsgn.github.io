01 · THE DECISIONS

Four choices that shape the screen

A tenant who is shopping for space behaves differently from one who already has a desk. These are the calls I made for that visitor.

The map comes first

People think in buildings, not unit IDs. "The one by the bus stop" is a better handle than "B-3.2". The map is the filter result you can see, and each roof shows how many units match.

The assistant shows its guesses

It turns 35 people into 25 desks. That is a guess, so it sits in chips you can read and fix. Change one and the ranking moves. The result stays a proposal until you apply it.

Monthly total, not €/m²

A price per square metre hides the number a finance lead asks for. Cards lead with the monthly total, rent plus service charge, and keep the rate small beside it.

A stacking plan per building

It shows what is left and how each floor splits. A tenant can see that a 280 m² unit sits on top of a leased floor without reading a table. Status uses pattern and text, never colour alone.

02 · EDGE CASES I DESIGNED

The states a demo usually skips

Each button below resets the prototype above and jumps to that state.

No matches

Says what to change and gives one tap to do it, for example "Widen the area to 60-300 m² to see 1 unit".

Filter error

Minimum above maximum, or letters in a number field. The error sits under the field and the area filter pauses.

Shortlist full

A fourth unit gets a clear message and a link to the shortlist, not a silent refusal.

Reserved while viewing

Another company reserves the unit while you read it. The page says "Reserved 2 min ago", disables booking and offers similar units.

Building under construction

Quay is on the map but has no units. It shows its ready date and a way to follow it.

Slot conflict

"10:30 was just taken." The form names two free times and books one in a tap.

03 · NEW COMPONENTS

What I added to the Brightyard system

These four did not exist in by.css. Each is built from system tokens and rendered live below. They are used in the prototype above.

Isometric campus map

A canvas drawing that reads its colours from the tokens and redraws on theme change. The roof badge carries the count of matching units.

Stacking plan bar

One floor, split into units by width in proportion to m². Status is a fill pattern plus a word in the legend, so it never depends on colour.

04 · WHAT I WOULD TEST

Three hypotheses

None of these has been tested. They are what I would check first with real prospective tenants.

Hypothesis 1

Map first shortens the search

Prospects who start from the map open fewer irrelevant units than prospects who start from a list. I would compare units opened per search.

Hypothesis 2

Visible assumptions earn trust

People who see and edit the desk guess accept the assistant's top pick more often than people who only see a ranked list. I would watch whether they touch the chips.

Hypothesis 3

Totals lead to more viewings

A monthly total on the card makes a finance reader comfortable enough to book a viewing without asking leasing for a quote first.
